There is a dish my mother makes that I have never been able to fully recreate. It is a simple soup — a clear broth with pork and lotus root — but the way she makes it tastes like something no recipe could ever write down. It tastes like her hands. Like her kitchen. Like home.

I started the Cambodian Cooking Collective because I am afraid of forgetting.

The Khmer Rouge killed more than two million people — a quarter of Cambodia's population. Among the things they tried to destroy was culture: language, art, religion, and food. The recipes that survived did so because people carried them in their bodies and their memory, passed down in kitchens and whispered in refugee camps.

Many of us in the diaspora grew up with fragments. Half a recipe. The memory of a smell. A dish we ate once and never knew the name of.

This collective is my attempt to gather those fragments together — to cook them, to share them, to make sure they survive another generation.

If you grew up eating Khmer food, I hope this is a place where you feel something familiar. And if you've never tasted it before, I hope it becomes something you love.

Come cook with us.
