ingredients:
  meats:
    - 500g firm white fish (snakehead or tilapia), sliced
  produce:
    - 4–6 banana leaves (for cups)
    - 1 cup fresh spinach or noni leaves
    - Kaffir lime leaves, thinly sliced, to garnish
    - 1–2 red chilies, sliced, to garnish
  pantry:
    - 400ml coconut milk
    - 3 tbsp kroeung paste
    - 2 eggs, beaten
    - 2 tbsp fish sauce
    - 1 tsp palm sugar
equipment:
  - Steamer or large pot with steaming rack
  - Mixing bowl
  - Banana leaf cups (or small ramekins)
  - Toothpicks or stapler for banana leaf cups
steps:
  - Make or prepare kroeung paste by blending together lemongrass, galangal, kaffir lime zest, turmeric, garlic, shallots, and dried chilies into a smooth paste.
  - In a bowl, combine coconut milk, kroeung paste, fish sauce, palm sugar, and beaten eggs. Mix well until smooth.
  - Add the fish slices and toss gently to coat.
  - Line each banana leaf cup with a few spinach or noni leaves. Ladle the fish and coconut mixture in, filling about three-quarters full.
  - Place cups in a steamer over boiling water. Steam for 20–25 minutes until the amok is set and firm.
  - Garnish with kaffir lime leaf strips and red chili. Serve immediately with steamed jasmine rice.
---

Amok is often called Cambodia's national dish. The word "amok" refers to the steaming technique — the dish is cooked gently in banana leaf cups, which give it a subtle earthy aroma. Kroeung paste is the soul of this dish; it takes time to make from scratch but the flavor is incomparable.

If you can't find banana leaves, small ramekins work just fine. The dish will taste the same — only the presentation changes.
