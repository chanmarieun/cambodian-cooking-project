ingredients:
  meats:
    - 500g beef (short rib or brisket), cut into chunks
    - 4 cups beef broth or water
  produce:
    - 2 stalks lemongrass, bruised and cut into 2-inch pieces
    - 3 kaffir lime leaves
    - 2 medium tomatoes, quartered
    - 1 cup pineapple chunks (fresh or canned)
    - 1 cup bean sprouts
    - 2–3 Thai chilies, sliced
    - Fresh cilantro and Thai basil, to garnish
    - 1 lime, cut into wedges
  pantry:
    - 3 tbsp kroeung paste (lemongrass, galangal, turmeric, garlic, shallots)
    - 3 tbsp tamarind paste
    - 2 tbsp fish sauce
    - 1 tsp palm sugar
    - 1 tbsp vegetable oil
equipment:
  - Large pot or Dutch oven
  - Mortar and pestle or blender (for kroeung)
  - Ladle
steps:
  - If making kroeung from scratch, pound together lemongrass, galangal, turmeric, garlic, and shallots in a mortar until a smooth paste forms.
  - Heat oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the kroeung paste and fry for 2–3 minutes until fragrant.
  - Add the beef and sear for 3–4 minutes, turning to coat in the paste.
  - Pour in the beef broth and add the lemongrass stalks and kaffir lime leaves. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer. Cook for 40–50 minutes until the beef is tender.
  - Stir in the tamarind paste, fish sauce, and palm sugar. Taste and adjust — it should be sour, savory, and slightly sweet.
  - Add the tomatoes and pineapple and simmer for another 5 minutes.
  - Remove from heat. Add bean sprouts and ladle into bowls.
  - Garnish with cilantro, Thai basil, sliced chili, and a squeeze of lime. Serve with steamed jasmine rice.

Samlor machu is one of the great Khmer sour soups — a category of dish that appears across Southeast Asia but takes on its own character in Cambodian cooking. The word "machu" means sour, and kroeung gives this version its deep, aromatic foundation.

The balance of sour, savory, and sweet is everything in this dish. Tamarind carries most of the sourness, but a squeeze of fresh lime at the table brings it alive. Pineapple is traditional — don't skip it. It softens as it cooks and adds a gentle sweetness that rounds out the broth.

This soup is best made with bone-in beef cuts like short rib, which give the broth more body and flavor.
